<KeyTakeaways>
- If people mostly **read and then contact you**, you need a website.
- If people need to **log in and get work done**, you need a platform.
- Many Nigerian businesses need a website now and a focused platform later. Plan the two so they can live side by side.
</KeyTakeaways>

Most technology decisions go wrong before any code is written. A business asks for "a website" when the real problem is an operational one, or commissions an ambitious "app" when a fast, credible website would have produced enquiries within weeks.

This guide gives you a practical way to tell the difference, using questions you can answer without technical knowledge.

## Start with the job, not the technology

Before you think about features, write one sentence describing what the investment must do for the business. Compare these:

- "Help companies in Lagos understand our services and request a quote." That is a **website**.
- "Let parents see their child's results and pay school fees without calling the office." That is a **platform**.
- "Let customers browse products, pay and track delivery." That is an **online store**, a specialised platform.

The first is mainly *communication*. The second and third are mainly *operations*. That one distinction drives almost every other decision: scope, cost, timeline and who needs to maintain it.

## Five questions that settle it

Answer these honestly. Two or more "yes" answers usually point to a platform.

1. **Do users need their own account?** Logging in, saved information and personal dashboards are platform territory.
2. **Do different people need different permissions?** For example admins, staff, customers, parents or vendors.
3. **Does the system store data that changes every day?** Orders, attendance, bookings, results, invoices.
4. **Does money or a regulated process move through it?** Payments, subscriptions, approvals, records you must audit.
5. **Would the business stop working if it went down for a day?** If yes, you are buying operational software, and it needs to be built and supported like it.

If every answer is "no", a well-built website is almost certainly the better investment. Spend the difference on content, photography and search visibility.

## What a website does well

A modern business website earns trust and generates demand. Done properly it:

- explains what you do and who it is for in seconds;
- answers the questions buyers ask before they call;
- works fast on the phones and data connections your customers actually use;
- is structured so Google can understand and rank each service;
- makes the next step obvious: call, WhatsApp, quote request or booking.

## What a custom platform does well

A platform is software shaped around how your work actually happens. It is the right choice when the cost of the current process is high: staff time lost to repetitive admin, errors from re-typing data, delayed payments or customers who cannot get answers without calling.

Examples from our own work include [SchoolKit](https://schoolkit.ng), which connects fees, attendance, results, admissions and parent communication for schools, and data-driven publishing platforms such as [BetGenius](https://www.betgenius.ng). Read more about [custom platforms and portals](/services/custom-platforms/).

Typical platform types:

| Platform | What it replaces |
| --- | --- |
| Customer or staff portal | Email threads, phone calls, paper forms |
| Marketplace or directory | Manual matching of buyers and providers |
| SaaS product | A service you want to sell to many businesses |
| Workflow or operations tool | Spreadsheets and WhatsApp coordination |

## The middle path most businesses should take

You rarely need to choose one forever. A sensible sequence is:

1. **Launch the website first.** Prove demand, sharpen your positioning and start building search visibility.
2. **Map the workflow that hurts most.** Write down every step, who does it and where it breaks.
3. **Build the smallest platform that removes that pain end to end.** One complete workflow beats ten half-finished features.
4. **Connect the two.** Keep the marketing website and the application separate but linked, for example `yourbusiness.ng` and `app.yourbusiness.ng`, so each can evolve at its own pace.

This protects your budget and gives the platform real users and real requirements from day one.

## Questions to ask any agency before you commit

- What would you build first, and what would you deliberately leave out?
- Who owns the code, domain, hosting and data at the end of the project?
- How will the system be supported, updated and backed up after launch?
- How does it perform on a mid-range Android phone on a mobile network?
- What will we measure to know the investment is working?

A good partner gives clear, specific answers and is comfortable telling you when you need *less* technology than you asked for.
